# ThePod — Marketing Video Script

**Format:** Vertical or landscape, ~42s. **Tone:** Premium, confident,
product-first — the register of an iPod or Walkman launch ad, not a "look
what I hacked together" reveal. ThePod is presented as a finished, standalone
consumer product. **Do not mention Raspberry Pi, or any internal component
brand/part name, anywhere in VO or on-screen text** — no "reveal," no
DIY/homemade framing. The internals are exactly as invisible to the viewer
as an iPhone's chipset is in an Apple ad. **Music:** Warm, upbeat, restrained
electronic/indie-pop bed — a product-launch track, not a hype-reel blast.

---

### SCENE 1 — Hero (0:00–0:05)

**Visual:** Clean hero shot of the physical device — in hand, or resting on
a minimal surface. Slow rotation or a single confident push-in, showing off
the enclosure's finish and shape. Premium product-photography lighting.

**On-screen text:**
> "ThePod."

**VO:**
> "Music, the way it was recorded."

**Audio:** Music bed begins under the push-in, no lead-in silence.

---

### SCENE 2 — Design (0:05–0:11)

**Visual:** Close-up detail shots: the enclosure's edges, the USB-C port,
the weight of it turning over in a hand. Tactile, unhurried.

**VO:**
> "Solid. Pocketable. Built to last as long as your music does."

**Audio:** Bed continues, steady.

---

### SCENE 3 — Connect (0:11–0:17)

**Visual:** The companion app's Pod tab — scanning, "ThePod" appears, tap to
connect, the accent indicator lights, "Connected" badge. Real pairing flow,
shot straight — fast and effortless.

**VO:**
> "Pairs in seconds. No cables to your phone. No cloud in between."

**Audio:** Soft tap sound on connect, light chime on "Connected."

---

### SCENE 4 — Playback (0:17–0:25)

**Visual:** Library → tap a song → Now Playing screen. Album art fades in.
Quality badge reads "FLAC · 24-BIT · 96 kHz." Play/pause tapped, seek bar
advances.

**VO:**
> "Lossless, start to finish. Every detail your music actually has."

**Audio:** Soft swell on the album art fade-in; faint tick as the seek bar
fills.

---

### SCENE 5 — The Details (0:25–0:34)

**Visual:** Quick cuts: EQ presets switching (Flat → Bass → Vocal), battery
percentage, storage bar, WiFi network name. Fast, confident cuts, under a
second each.

**VO:**
> "Real EQ. Real battery life. Room for your whole library."

**Audio:** Quick matched ticks per cut, bed at its fullest presence here.

---

### SCENE 6 — Outro / Tagline (0:34–0:40)

**Visual:** Cut to black. Centered type, two lines landing in sequence,
each held ~1.3–1.5s:

> "ThePod."
> "Your music. Untouched."

Small wordmark in the app's own palette (near-black `#0A0A0A` background,
red-orange `#ec3013` accent) holds on the last beat.

**Audio:** Music swells softly under the final line, then fades to silence.

---

### SCENE 7 — Endcard (0:40–0:42)

**Visual:** Wordmark holds, small caption beneath:

> "Yours to hold."

Optional CTA line beneath (fill in once sales channel/pricing is set —
leave this off entirely rather than guess a channel or price):
> "[Where to get one]"

**Audio:** Silence, or a single soft final note.

---

## Alternate 15s cutdown (for social/Stories)

Use Scenes 1 (trimmed), 4, and 6:
1. Hero — device push-in, "ThePod." (3s)
2. Playback — album art + FLAC badge, seek bar filling (6s)
3. Outro — two-line tagline, tight timing (4s)

## Notes for whoever shoots/renders this

- **No Raspberry Pi mention, no internal spec reveal, no "it's actually
  just a ___" beat anywhere.** Treat this exactly like an Apple or Sony
  product launch — the audience doesn't need to know what's inside a
  premium device to want one, and it undercuts the product's positioning to
  frame it as a hack rather than a finished product.
- Shoot the physical device with real product photography, not just screen
  recordings — Scenes 1 and 2 carry the "this is a real, well-made object"
  weight, which now has to come entirely from how the hardware looks and
  feels on camera.
- The app screens (Scenes 3–5) should use real screens and a real Bluetooth
  connection, not static mockups — but nothing on-screen should reference
  the underlying hardware platform either (no spec cards, no hardware/about
  screens naming components).
- Wired IEMs are bring-your-own — don't show or imply they're in the box.
- Keep VO lines short and confident. If a line doesn't survive being cut in
  half, cut it in half.
- Don't oversell in copy what the product doesn't do yet (see
  `PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md` for the current, accurate feature list).
- Sales channel and price aren't finalized — leave the endcard CTA blank
  rather than inventing a link, price, or store name.
